The simulation first, and easy means right

The time goes to the simulation and to how easily it gives historical results. Easy means right, not the least code.

Why
The outcome is decided in the simulation; the picture explains it. A result that is wrong in an easy way is no help.
Why we believe in it now
This is where the questions that matter are asked (does this match what happened?) and where the method of sources and tests works.
What would make us think again
If the game became unusable because one cannot see what happens, the balance would shift toward the picture.

The truth comes from the sources

Every value carries its source and its uncertainty; what lacks a source is marked MOCK. A value is never adjusted to make a result come out right. A new vehicle should give fair results from its own data, and more detail means more data, never a new rule.

Why
A simulation tuned until it looks right teaches nothing about the past; one built from sources can be checked, and its deviations have reasons.
Why we believe in it now
The sources at hand (field manuals, Bird & Livingston, Chamberlain & Doyle, the technical manuals, the war diaries) are enough to build on, and the marks show where they are not.
What would make us think again
Where the sources run out, the sensitivity decides: if the uncertainty barely changes the outcome it does not matter; if it does, finding the source comes first.

The same input gives the same result

WEGO and determinism: all chance comes from a seeded draw, the arithmetic gives the same bits on every machine, and a game can be played back exactly.

Why
Without it nothing can be tested, compared or explained. A replay is a proof: it shows what happened, not something like it.
Why we believe in it now
It holds today across machines, and every rule since has been built on it.
What would make us think again
Nothing foreseen; giving it up would undo the tests and the replays together.

The tools are the judge

Tests, git and cargo decide what is true, not memory. A test is broken on purpose to show it can go red. A green suite that never ran the code is a fault of its own.

Why
It has gone wrong before: tests green for the wrong reason, the shooter's silhouette passed instead of the target's, firing code that never ran while every test was green.
Why we believe in it now
With a thousand tests and code written by agents from specifications, the tests are what lets a change be trusted.
What would make us think again
If the tests became so slow or brittle that they were skipped; then they would be mended, not dropped.

The crew knows only what it sees

Each side has its own knowledge: its units and what they have seen. The sitrep is that view and is tested for leaks, and the opponent is a player outside the engine with the same view.

Why
The uncertainty of a fight is part of the history. An opponent that knows what it cannot see gives the wrong results.
Why we believe in it now
The side views, the sitrep and the leak test exist, so the rule can be kept as the opponent grows.
What would make us think again
Only a scenario's own intelligence, given openly in the briefing, not a leak from the engine.

A 3D world drawn in 2D

The simulation reckons in 2.5D (heights, sight lines, the hull's tilt, the shot's angle); the window is 2D from above, with shading, contours, figures and views that explain.

Why
Full 3D as the big studios make it needs artists and animators; 3D within our means would be crude and invite a comparison it always loses. A 3D world drawn in 2D can look good through style, rules instead of hand-drawn art, and information first, and it explains a result better.
Why we believe in it now
It is a way forward to something that can be built, not a fallback.
What would make us think again
If a vertical effect could not be explained in 2D at all; the answer would first be a new 2D view.

Nothing is final, and the journey is the point

A decision was the best with what was known then, and any decision may be questioned. No single battle or front is the goal, so a solution is tried against every front.

Why
Questioning is how the simulation gets better, and the fun is in the building, not in being done.
Why we believe in it now
The site keeps each decision with its reason and its date, so taking one up again costs little and loses nothing.
What would make us think again
This one is the pillar that lets the others change.
